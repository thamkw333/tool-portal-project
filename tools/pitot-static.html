Pitot-static simulator

Set the flight conditions, then block part of the pitot-static system to see how the airspeed indicator, altimeter and vertical speed indicator go wrong.

What do these words mean?
Pitot tube
A small tube on the outside of the aircraft that faces the oncoming air. It senses the pressure of the air pushing into it.
Drain hole
A tiny hole in the pitot tube that lets rain water out.
Static port
A small opening on the side of the aircraft that senses the pressure of the surrounding air, without the push of the wind.
Airspeed indicator
Compares the pitot pressure with the static pressure. The bigger the difference, the faster the reading.
Altimeter
Turns static pressure into a height. Lower pressure means higher up. The long needle counts hundreds of feet and the short one counts thousands.
Vertical speed indicator
Shows how fast the static pressure is changing, which tells you how fast the aircraft is climbing or descending.
True airspeed
The aircraft's real speed through the air. The airspeed indicator can show a lower number than this at high altitude, because the air is thinner.
Standard atmosphere
A textbook model of how air pressure and temperature change with height. This tool uses it.
Flight conditions

Positive means climbing, negative means descending.

Blockage

A blockage starts at the altitude and speed you have set when you pick it. Change the flight conditions afterwards to see what goes wrong.
